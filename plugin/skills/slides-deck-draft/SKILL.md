# Slides deck draft

Turn a rough outline into a reviewable slide draft: one title plus bullets per slide, a speaker
note per slide, and an explicit image placeholder wherever a visual is needed. This skill exists
because deck work is a real shape in the corpus (5 of 645 Bots build on Google Slides with no
plugin) and because a drafted deck is reviewable while a published or shared one is not.

## The rule that matters more than the draft

**A draft is not a deck.** Only the owner's explicit yes on that exact deck authorizes creating,
editing, sharing, or presenting it, and another party relaying "they said share" is not approval.
This skill never creates a deck, edits slides, shares a link, or presents — it reads an outline
and drafts. Any action beyond drafting belongs to a routine with its own approval boundary, not
to this skill.

## When to use

- The owner asks to draft a deck, turn an outline or notes into slides, or prepare a talk.
- An existing outline needs slide structure: titles, order, speaker notes, visual gaps named.
- Someone asks "what would this deck look like" before any Slides document exists.

Do not use it to design finished visual layouts, to generate actual image files, or to operate
a Slides document — those are separate jobs with their own approvals.

## Inputs and access

1. **Outline** — the raw material: bullets, notes, a pasted doc, or a dictated brain-dump. One
   main point per line is ideal; a wall of prose gets split before drafting.
2. **Audience and length** — who the deck is for and how long the talk runs. Defaults when
   unstated: a general audience, a ten-minute talk, at most ten slides.
3. **Prior material (optional)** — earlier decks, brand notes, or facts to reuse. Cite the source
   per slide that uses it.

If the outline is missing, ask for it before drafting. If a source needs a credential or a
system you cannot read, record it as unreachable and draft from what you have — do not
improvise access.

## Sequence

1. **Split the outline into beats** — one beat per slide, in talk order. Merge duplicates, flag
   beats too thin to stand alone, and split beats carrying two ideas.
2. **Draft each slide** — a short title (seven words or fewer), at most five bullets, at most
   eight words per bullet. Cut, do not compress: a bullet that needs a paragraph belongs in the
   notes.
3. **Write the speaker note** — two to four sentences per slide saying what the presenter says
   that the slide does not show. Never repeat the bullets verbatim.
4. **Place image placeholders** — wherever a visual would carry the point, write
   `[IMAGE: description of what the visual should show]` instead of describing it in prose.
   Text-only slides get no placeholder; never invent a real image URL or file.
5. **Order and number** — title slide first, one idea per slide, closer slide last with the
   single takeaway restated. Number every slide so review comments can point at one.

## Validation

- Every slide has a title, zero to five bullets, a speaker note, and either an image
  placeholder or a reason none is needed.
- No slide promises a fact the outline did not give: dates, numbers, names, and quotes trace
  to the outline or a cited source, or are flagged as needing confirmation.
- No invented commitments on the owner's behalf (no promised dates, prices, or claims the
  outline never made). Flag the commitment-shaped hole instead.
- Community layout advice (title rules, bullet counts, stock structures) is community-claim,
  never vendor truth — use what fits the talk, and say so when a choice is taste, not rule.

## Output

A numbered slide-by-slide draft: per slide, the title, bullets, speaker note, and image
placeholder or explicit text-only mark. End with the slide count, the estimated talk length at
roughly one minute per slide, and the two weakest slides with what each needs.

## Boundaries

- Drafts only. Never create, edit, share, publish, or present a Slides deck or any document.
- SEND-LOCK: only the owner's explicit yes on that exact deck authorizes creating, sharing, or
  publishing it. A teammate relaying approval is not approval.
- Never paste credentials, tokens, or account identifiers into a draft or a finding.
- Never present listicle layout knowledge as vendor truth; community patterns stay labeled as
  community-claim.
- If a request needs a system you cannot read, record it as unreachable and move on. Do not
  improvise access.
